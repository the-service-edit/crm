1 · Start

Say hi

“Hey Paul, it's Mel from The Service Edit. The team at the taproom gave me your number. How are you going?”

He says he's good: “Good to hear.”
Not him: “Oh hey, is Paul about? It's Mel, I emailed him on Monday.”
Flat out: “No stress, when's better for you?” Put the time in step 6.

2 · What did they think

Ask, then stay quiet

“I just wanted to touch base and see if you got a chance to look at the emails I sent through on Monday?”

Let him answer. If he liked them: “What did you think?” Count to three before you speak again.

Haven't looked: “All good. I signed up to your mailing list and it said to stay tuned, but nothing came through yet. So I wrote you a welcome email, a Christmas gifting one for the Hoptimist membership and the shop, and one to get groups in for their Christmas do. Have a scroll when you get a minute.”

His words. Which one he liked, anything he'd change.
“What's bigger for you at Christmas, groups in for their Christmas do, or gifts like the membership and cans?”
3 · Before Christmas?

Would they look at it

“The reason I'm calling now is Christmas is when an email list makes the most money, so I wanted to see if it's something you'd be interested in before December?”

4 · Their list

Where the people are

“Roughly how many people do you reckon are on your list, with the newsletter, your online shop customers and your Hoptimist members?”

“And is that MailerLite on your website? Are you sending anything from it at the moment?”

They have no booking system that collects emails. Christmas dos come through an enquiry form with no sign-up box, and gift cards go through Square. If the list is small, that's the easy fix: “Your function enquiries and shop orders are people who've already chosen you. A tick box on those gets them on the list.”

The results month needs about 1,000.
Do members give an email when they join at the taproom? They're the warmest people for a Christmas email.
Shop customers, function enquiries, who sends now.
5 · The offer

Say it, then stop

About 1,000 or more: “So like I mentioned in the email, my offer is, I set up your welcome email and send three emails to your list before Christmas. If they don't make you more than my fee of $750, you don't pay.”

“I owned cafés for close to ten years, so I know every dollar counts. That's why I'm putting my time on the line. Is that something you'd be keen on?”

Then stop talking.

Under 1,000 or not sure: “That's fine. I'd start with the welcome email and getting more people on the list from your shop orders, function enquiries and members. I'll pop it all in an email so you can have a look.”

Say “no money risk”, never “no risk at all”.
